MIMIC-Sepsis

A Curated Benchmark for Modeling and Learning from Sepsis Trajectories in the ICU

Yong Huang, Zhongqi Yang, Amir M. Rahmani

IEEE-EMBS International Conference on Biomedical and Health Informatics (BHI) 2025

Paper (arXiv) Code (GitHub)

Abstract

We present MIMIC-Sepsis, a curated cohort derived from MIMIC-IV containing 35,239 ICU patients with time-aligned clinical variables and standardized treatment data. We describe a transparent preprocessing pipeline based on Sepsis-3 criteria and release benchmark tasks for mortality prediction, length-of-stay estimation, and shock onset classification. Results show that incorporating treatment variables substantially improves model performance, particularly for Transformer architectures.

Motivation

Sepsis causes approximately 48.9 million cases and 11 million deaths annually worldwide, representing 20% of global deaths. Despite advances in machine learning for critical care, existing sepsis prediction research faces three key challenges:

Outdated Datasets

Most studies rely on MIMIC-III, which may not reflect current ICU practices and treatment protocols.

Inconsistent Preprocessing

Ad-hoc, poorly documented data curation makes results difficult to reproduce and compare across studies.

Missing Treatment Data

Clinical interventions (vasopressors, fluids, antibiotics) are frequently overlooked in trajectory analyses despite their critical influence on patient outcomes.

Sepsis-3 Definition

Infection onset is identified as the earliest timepoint where antibiotic administration occurs within 24 hours before a positive culture, or a culture is obtained within 72 hours before antibiotic administration. Sepsis is defined as a SOFA score increase ≥ 2 from baseline in the presence of infection. Septic shock requires adequate fluid resuscitation (≥ 2000 mL in prior 12h), MAP < 65 mmHg, vasopressor requirement, and lactate > 2 mmol/L.

Clinical Variables

Vital Signs (46)

Heart rate, blood pressure (systolic, diastolic, mean), respiratory rate, SpO2, temperature, ventilator parameters

Laboratory Values (42+)

Electrolytes, coagulation panel, blood gases, cardiac markers, liver/renal function, CBC

Interventions

Vasopressors (norepinephrine-equivalent), IV fluids (tonicity-corrected), antibiotics, mechanical ventilation

Derived Scores

SOFA (6 organ systems), SIRS, P/F ratio, shock index, Charlson Comorbidity Index

Benchmark Tasks

Four predictive tasks reflect real-world challenges in sepsis care, grouped by temporal structure:

Static Prediction (Early Observations)

Using only the first 6 hours of the sepsis trajectory to simulate early clinical decision-making:

TaskTypeDescriptionMetrics
In-Hospital Mortality (IHM) Binary Classification Predict whether the patient will survive to discharge AUROC, AUPRC
Length of Stay (LOS) Regression Predict ICU length of stay in days RMSE, MAE

Dynamic Prediction (Rolling Windows)

At each timestep, models observe the previous 6 hours to predict event occurrence within the next 24 hours:

TaskTypeDescriptionMetrics
Vasopressor Requirement (VR) Binary Classification Predict whether vasopressors will be needed in the next 24h AUROC, AUPRC
Septic Shock (SS) Binary Classification Predict septic shock onset within the next 24h AUROC, AUPRC

Key Findings

  • Transformers consistently outperform linear and LSTM baselines across all tasks
  • Treatment variables are critical for dynamic tasks — LSTM gains +0.112 AUROC and +0.235 AUPRC for vasopressor requirement when treatment data is included
  • Static tasks are less affected by treatment variables due to the limited 6-hour observation window
  • Temporal models benefit most from enriched treatment-aware representation

Model Architectures

Linear Baseline

Flattens 3D temporal data (N, T, X) to 2D (N, T×X) and applies scikit-learn models: LogisticRegression for classification, Ridge/Lasso/ElasticNet for regression.

LSTM

Two-layer PyTorch LSTM with hidden dimension 64, dropout 0.1, and Adam optimizer. Uses the final hidden state for prediction. Preserves sequential temporal dependencies.

Transformer Encoder

Multi-layer encoder with 8 self-attention heads, 2 layers, sine/cosine positional encoding, dropout 0.1, and Adam optimizer. Captures long-range dependencies without recurrence.

Discussion & Future Work

Limitations

Single-institution derivation (Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center) may affect generalizability. Retrospective EHR data is subject to documentation inconsistencies and care delivery biases. Only Sepsis-3 criteria are implemented; newer scoring tools (qSOFA) are not yet integrated.

Future Directions

Reinforcement learning for treatment personalization. Incorporation of unstructured clinical notes (discharge summaries, progress notes). Multimodal modeling combining structured EHR data with free text.
